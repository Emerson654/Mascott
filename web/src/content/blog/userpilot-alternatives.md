**Short answer:** the main Userpilot alternatives are **Appcues** (similar scope, similar price), **UserGuiding** and **Product Fruits** (cheaper tour builders), **Userflow** (a fast flow builder), **Chameleon** (deep customisation) and **AI onboarding guides** like Mascott, which replace hand-built tours with an assistant that answers users and points to the next step. If you have a product or growth person with time to build and maintain flows, and you want surveys and analytics in the same tool, Userpilot is still a very good choice.

## What Userpilot does well

Userpilot is one of the most complete product adoption platforms. You get no-code in-app flows, tooltips, onboarding checklists, a resource center, NPS and in-app surveys, plus product analytics and segmentation. It is also the content and SEO leader of its category, which is why it shows up first when you search for onboarding tools.

It is reported at roughly **$249-299 per month** ([costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/appcues-vs-userpilot/), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/179187/Userpilot/reviews/1154786)).

## Why small teams look elsewhere

- **Price at an early stage.** $250-300 a month is a large line for a team with a few hundred signups a month.
- **Building tours takes time.** Every flow is designed by hand. Someone has to own it.
- **Tours break.** When the UI changes, selectors and screenshots go stale.
- **Tours are linear.** A user who arrives with a specific question ("how do I import my CSV?") is pushed through steps they did not ask for, then skips the tour.
- **Overlap.** Many teams already pay for PostHog, Mixpanel or Amplitude for analytics.

## How to choose

Ask three questions before you open a pricing page:

1. **Who will build and maintain the onboarding?** If nobody has a few hours a week, favour tools that need little upkeep.
2. **Is your onboarding one fixed path, or many?** One path suits tours. Many paths (different roles, different goals) suit an assistant that adapts.
3. **What do you actually measure?** If the answer is "trial-to-activation", you need a tool that moves that number, not a tool with the most features.

## 1. Appcues

Appcues is the closest like-for-like alternative: modals, tooltips, checklists, banners and NPS, with a clean builder. It is reported at about $249 per month on annual billing, so it rarely saves money compared with Userpilot. Teams choose it for the builder experience. Full comparison: [Appcues alternatives](/alternatives/appcues).

## 2. UserGuiding

UserGuiding covers the core of onboarding (tours, hotspots, checklists, a resource center) and its Basic plan is reported at about $89 per month ([toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/userguiding/pricing)). It is the obvious pick if you want tours and need to cut the bill by two thirds.

## 3. Product Fruits

Product Fruits targets smaller budgets with tours, checklists, hints and a knowledge base. It ranks at the top of Google for "product tour software", which tells you its content is solid. We could not confirm current pricing from a reliable source, so check their site.

## 4. Userflow

Userflow is often shortlisted next to Userpilot and Appcues for its fast flow builder. It suits teams that change their onboarding every week.

## 5. Chameleon

Chameleon gives product teams fine control over the look and targeting of in-product experiences. It fits design-led teams more than small teams short on time.

## 6. Mascott (AI guide instead of tours)

Mascott takes a different route. Instead of scripting a tour, you give a mascot your help pages, docs and the key steps of your product. It then:

- greets new users and asks what they want to do;

## Tours vs an AI guide: which one?

| Situation | Better fit |
|---|---|
| One fixed path every user must see | Tour builder |
| Users arrive with different goals and questions | AI guide |
| Nobody has time to maintain flows | AI guide |
| You need NPS and in-app surveys | Userpilot, Appcues |
| UI changes often | AI guide (fewer selectors to break) |
| You want proof before buying | Any tool you can A/B test on new signups |

## How to test an alternative without risk

1. Pick **one activation event** (first project created, first integration connected).
2. Measure the current rate for new signups over two weeks.
3. Run the new tool on half of new signups for two weeks, keeping everything else the same.
4. Compare. Keep the tool only if activation moved, not because the demo looked good.

## FAQ

### What is the best Userpilot alternative for a startup?

For tours on a budget, UserGuiding (reported at ~$89/month). For no tours at all and an assistant that adapts to each user, an AI guide such as Mascott. For full scope, Appcues.

### Is Userpilot worth the price?

It is worth it if a dedicated person uses its flows, surveys and analytics every week. If you mostly need "help new users finish setup", cheaper options cover that.

### Can an AI guide replace product tours?

For many small SaaS products, yes, because users ask for help at the moment they need it. For compliance flows or must-see sequences, keep a short tour.
