**Short answer:** for a small SaaS team, the best Intercom alternative depends on the job you hired Intercom for. If it is support chat at a lower price, look at **Crisp** or **Tidio**. If it is an AI that answers from your docs, look at **Chatbase** or **CustomGPT.ai**. If it is getting new trial users to their first win, an **AI onboarding guide** such as Mascott is the closer fit. If you run a real support team with several agents, Intercom is probably still the right tool.

We make Mascott, so we have a bias. To keep this useful, every competitor price below comes from public third-party pricing guides (linked), dated October 2026, and we say plainly where other tools are better.

## Why small teams look for an Intercom alternative

Intercom is a strong product. Teams rarely leave because it does not work. They leave because of how it is priced and how much of it they actually use.

- **Per-resolution billing.** Fin, Intercom's AI agent, is reported at about **$0.99 per resolution**, and several guides mention a **50-resolution monthly minimum** ([Ringly](https://www.ringly.io/blog/intercom-pricing), [dragapp](https://dragapp.com/blog/intercom-pricing/)). One Capterra reviewer says it "adds up quickly at scale" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/134347/Intercom/reviews/)).
- **Seats.** The Essential plan is reported at **$29 per seat per month** on annual billing, $39 monthly. Every new teammate in the inbox adds to the bill.
- **Scope.** Inbox, ticketing, help center, outbound and workflows are built for support teams. A three-person startup often uses the messenger and little else.
- **Job mismatch.** Many early SaaS teams installed Intercom to "help users", when the real problem was that trial users never finished setup. A support inbox does not fix that.

## How to choose: four questions

Before comparing tools, answer these. They will remove half the list.

1. **Do you need humans in the loop?** If yes, you need a shared inbox (Crisp, Tidio, Intercom). If no, a pure AI tool may be enough.
2. **Is your main goal resolving tickets or activating new users?** Support tools optimise for the first. Onboarding tools optimise for the second.
3. **How do you want to pay?** Per seat, per resolution, per conversation, per credit, or a flat fee. Bill anxiety is the most common complaint about this category.
4. **Who will maintain it?** Chatbot flows and product tours need upkeep. AI tools trained on your docs need good docs.

## 1. Crisp: the closest like-for-like replacement

Crisp covers what most small teams use Intercom for: a chat widget, a shared inbox, a knowledge base and an AI assistant. Its Mini plan is reported at about $45 per month, Essentials at $95 and Plus at $295, with AI usage sold as monthly credits that do not roll over ([featurebase](https://www.featurebase.app/blog/crisp-pricing)).

**Pick Crisp if** you want to keep human chat and cut the bill. **Watch out for** AI credits: if your volume is spiky, unused credits are lost and heavy months cost more.

## 2. Tidio + Lyro: cheapest way to start

Tidio has a free plan (50 conversations a month plus 50 lifetime Lyro conversations) and a Starter plan reported at $29 per month for 100 conversations. Lyro, the AI agent, is an add-on from $39 per month for 50 AI conversations ([dragapp](https://www.dragapp.com/blog/tidio-pricing/)).

**Pick Tidio if** you sell online or need to start at zero. **Watch out for** conversation counting (a two-message chat counts the same as a twenty-message one) and reports of automatic upgrades during traffic spikes ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/tidio-reviews)). We compare it in more detail in [Tidio alternatives](/blog/tidio-alternatives).

## 3. Chatbase: a docs chatbot in an afternoon

If all you want is an AI that answers from your docs, Chatbase is quick to set up. Hobby is reported at about $40 monthly or $32 on annual billing, with 500 credits and 5 AI actions; branding removal is about $99 per month extra ([chatarmin](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/chatbase-pricing)).

**Pick Chatbase if** you want a low-cost docs bot and do not need a human inbox. **Watch out for** credits and the branding fee. See our three-way comparison: [Chatbase vs Intercom Fin vs an AI mascot](/blog/chatbase-vs-intercom-fin-vs-ai-mascot).

## 4. CustomGPT.ai: docs chatbot with more control

CustomGPT.ai Standard is reported at $99 per month and Premium at $499, billed by query quota ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10004844/CustomGPT-ai/pricing/)). It suits teams with large documentation sets who want a citation-heavy answer bot.

## 5. Mascott: when the real problem is activation

Mascott is not a helpdesk. It is an AI guide with a face that lives on your site or in your app, answers from your pages and docs, and **points new users to their next step**, for example by highlighting the button they need. It is priced flat ({{price:starter}} to {{price:scale}} per month), with soft conversation caps and no per-resolution fee.

**Pick Mascott if** trial users sign up and then stall, and you want a guide rather than another chat window. Many teams keep Intercom or Crisp for human support and add Mascott on top: there is nothing to migrate.

**Do not pick Mascott if** you need a shared inbox, ticketing, SLAs or a help center. Mascott is also new (launched 2026) and has no public reviews yet, which matters if you need a proven vendor.

## When you should stay on Intercom

Staying is the right call more often than "alternatives" articles admit:

- You have **five or more people** answering customers and need routing, SLAs and reporting.
- You use Intercom's **outbound** messages and product tours together with support.
- Fin already **resolves a high share** of tickets and the per-resolution cost is lower than the agent time it saves.

In those cases the fix is usually configuration (better help articles, fewer seats), not a migration.

## A simple way to decide this week

1. Export last month's conversations and tag them: **support** (something is broken or billing), **how-to** (where is X, how do I do Y) and **sales**.
2. If most are **support**, compare Crisp, Tidio and Intercom on price per seat.
3. If most are **how-to** from new users, your problem is onboarding. Try an AI guide like Mascott on your signup flow for two weeks and compare activation for new users with and without it.
4. If most are **sales**, keep a human-staffed chat and make sure it is easy to reach.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest Intercom alternative?

Tidio has a free plan and a $29 Starter plan, and Crisp has a free plan for two seats (both reported, October 2026). For AI answers only, Chatbase starts at about $32-40 per month.

### Is there an Intercom Fin alternative without per-resolution pricing?

Yes. Crisp sells AI as monthly credits, Chatbase as message credits, and Mascott as a flat monthly price with soft conversation caps.

### Can I use an AI onboarding guide together with Intercom?

Yes. An onboarding guide handles "where do I click" questions in the product and hands off to your Intercom messenger when a human is needed.

### Is Crisp better than Intercom?

For small teams on a budget, often yes. For larger support teams that need advanced routing, reporting and integrations, Intercom is usually stronger.
