# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers already pay for this category.** Site owners already budget monthly for chat widgets (Intercom, Crisp, Tidio). Intercom's Fin already charges per resolution ([intercom.com/pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)), which shows buyers accept paying for each outcome.
- **"Acts on the site" points at an outcome a buyer can count:** a booked slot, a started checkout, a filled form. Better than "answers questions".
- **One house style and one shared animation set keep the mascot cheap and quick to deliver.** Cheap to give, valuable to receive.
- **The 0 EUR in 3 months kill rule is healthy.** It forces selling before polishing.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No chosen market, so no offer.** Five segments, each with different pain, budget and channels. Indie hackers have no money. Showcase sites have no measurable pain. Agencies resell, they don't buy. Only e-commerce and SaaS have a big pain they can put a number on (abandoned carts, failed onboarding). "Everyone with a website" is the most common reason this lens passes.
2. **The mascot is the headline, but it is not the outcome.** Nobody's pain is "my widget isn't cute". Leading with the mascot makes this a nicer-looking chatbot, competing on price at 19-29 EUR against tools that already exist. The mascot is a bonus. "More checkouts / more activated users" is the outcome to sell.
3. **Likelihood and effort are both weak.** There is no proof the agent converts. Acting on a live site needs setup, testing and trust that it won't break things, which means high effort and low perceived likelihood. And every conversation costs money in LLM fees, so a cheap flat plan can lose money on the busiest customers.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one segment has the pain and the money? Name 10 real companies in it you could contact this week.
- What metric does the buyer care about, and what lift can you promise?
- Why would anyone believe it works before you have a single case study?
- How long from the snippet being pasted to the first completed action: minutes, or a week?
- What is the guarantee? Can you price on outcomes (per booking, per assisted checkout)?
- What does one conversation and one action cost you in inference? Nobody knows yet; all pricing depends on it.
- Could you charge 3x the current hypothesis to one segment and still be the obvious choice?
- SEO/GEO takes 2-3 months to bring traffic, and the kill rule fires at 3 months. Where does the first euro come from?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **One segment, one outcome, chosen before any code is written.** Candidates to test: Shopify stores (find a product, start checkout) or small SaaS (walk new users to activation). Pick one, not both.
2. **Rebuild the offer as a stack:** done-for-you install, an on-brand mascot within 24 hours, prebuilt actions for that platform, a weekly report of the conversions it drove, and a conditional guarantee (a measurable lift in 30 days or the month is refunded). Check every item against the inference cost.
3. **Presell.** Get 5-10 paying pilots or deposits from direct outreach within 30 days, priced on the value of the outcome, not on what widgets charge. SEO/GEO runs alongside as the slow channel. It is not the plan for the first euro.
4. **Measure the cost per conversation on a prototype** and set quotas so the heaviest users still bring in more than they cost.
