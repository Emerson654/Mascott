# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **Small slots really are open.** Most chatbot widgets only talk. An agent that also *acts* on the page (navigates, fills forms, starts a checkout) and has a recognisable character is a real difference from Intercom, Crisp and Tidio.
- **A brand could become a moat.** It is the one durable advantage a two-person team could build. Locking every mascot into one house style is the right move for that.
- **The visual layer is cheap.** One shared animation set keeps generating a mascot cheap and consistent.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret, no 10x, and a crowded market.** Every layer of this product is already sold within reach of the same buyer:
   - Agents that act on the page: [Pickaxe agent page control](https://pickaxe.co/learn/changelog/agent-page-control), [AIsuru web actions](https://docs.aisuru.com/en/advanced-features/web-interfaces-creating-interactive-experiences).
   - Embodied avatar agents: [Anam widget](https://docs.anam.ai/widget/overview), [Famulor AI Avatar](https://www.famulor.io/feature/ai-avatar), [Sentifyd](https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/sentifyd-ai-3d-avatars) (it claims actions through MCP).
   - Mascot avatars for agents: Mascotbot, [Mascofast](https://peerlist.io/dikjain/project/mascofast--ai-mascot-generator).
   - Incumbents with distribution: [Intercom Fin at $0.99 per resolution](https://stripe.com/customers/fin-ai).

   The mascot is a skin and the agent is a commodity built on third-party LLMs. One public data point makes this worse: an [AI mascot platform listed for sale](https://app.acquire.com/startup/d6jmha7vmf-ai-mascot-creation-and-animation-platform-for-brands-and-developers) reports 683 registered users but only 17 purchases. I would expect a copycat by month six, or an incumbent shipping "custom avatar" as a settings toggle.
2. **The distribution plan contradicts the kill rule.** SEO and GEO take 2-3 months to start producing traffic, and the founder's rule says the business is dead at 3 months with 0 EUR. So the only acquisition channel matures about when the deadline hits. There is no answer to "how do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost?"
3. **No small market to own.** The brief lists five or more segments, from indie hackers to Shopify stores, agencies, Wix sites and edtech. That is no beachhead at all. Spread across all of them, this business holds a tiny share of a huge, contested market, which is the opposite of what this lens wants. Variable inference costs at 19-29 EUR also risk thin margins.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know that Pickaxe, Anam, Sentifyd and Intercom do not?
- Which single segment could you own most of within 12 months, and how many buyers are in it? The market size is unknown; give it a number.
- Why now? What changed recently that makes this work today?
- What stops an incumbent from adding "upload your mascot" in one sprint?
- How do the first 10 paying customers arrive *before* SEO matures, and what is the CAC? That number is unknown.
- What is the inference cost of an average conversation plus its actions, and the gross margin at Starter? Both are unknown.
- Is a bootstrapped team of two with about 5,000 EUR the right team to fight funded startups and Intercom?
- Ten years out, what still holds?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable: the vote is PASS. These things would move it to FUND IF:
- Pick one niche small enough to dominate, for example a single platform's app store where a "cute shopping agent" can rank first.
- Show 10 pre-paid commitments from that niche *before* building, through direct outreach.
- Name a defensible wedge: deep native actions on one platform that general tools do not do, plus a brand style buyers ask for by name.
- Show a gross margin above about 70% at the entry price.
